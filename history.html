Brief History of Region IX

Zamboanga Peninsula holds a unique place in Philippine history. Indigenous seafaring peoples like the Subanens, Yakan, and Sama-Bajau inhabited the coastal lands for centuries prior to foreign settlement, establishing thriving maritime trade routes across the Sulu Sea.

During the 17th century, Spanish forces established Real Fuerza de San José (now Fort Pilar) as a military stronghold. This colonial presence sparked the development of Chavacano—a Spanish-based creole language still spoken across Zamboanga today as a vibrant symbol of cultural identity.

In the late 19th century, Dapitan City served as the exile home for Dr. Jose Rizal from 1892 to 1896. During his exile, Rizal contributed significantly as a physician, engineer, teacher, and artist, leaving behind historical landmarks that continue to inspire visitors today.

Cultural Highlights

Major Festivals

  • Zamboanga Hermosa Festival: Grand October festival honoring Our Lady of the Pillar.
  • Kinabayo Festival: Annual event in Dapitan celebrating historic traditions.
  • Regatta de Zamboanga: Colorful race of native vintas with vibrant sails.

Traditional Crafts & Foods

  • Yakan Weaving: Intricate handwoven textiles crafted by local artisans.
  • Curacha in Alavar Sauce: Legendary deep-sea spanner crab dish.
  • Brassware & Metalcraft: Intricate traditional brassware crafted in the region.
